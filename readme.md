# HNI Portfolio Review & Wealth Management Analysis


## Overview

This project looks at a ₹3.57 Cr HNI investment portfolio.

The portfolio has 21 holdings across Indian equities, US equities and ETFs, fixed income, commodities, crypto and cash.

The question was simple:

**Does the portfolio actually fit the client's needs?**

To answer that, the portfolio was reviewed from the client's point of view — starting with their goals and ending with what should be reviewed, changed or monitored.


---

## The Client

The analysis starts with the client, not the investments.

The case considers:

- ₹3.57 Cr of investible assets
- A 10+ year investment horizon
- A ₹35.4 lakh cash requirement within 3–4 years
- Existing business and property exposure
- Existing liabilities
- Different risk requirements for different financial goals


---

## The Three Goals

The same portfolio was tested against three different situations.


### 01 — Long-Term Wealth Creation

A 10–15 year horizon allows the portfolio to take more equity risk.

The focus here is growth, while keeping unnecessary concentration under control.


### 02 — Retirement / Financial Independence

The portfolio needs to become more dependable as the money gets closer to being used.

Risk and concentration therefore matter more than simply chasing returns.


### 03 — ₹35 Lakh Near-Term Requirement

Money needed in 3–4 years cannot depend heavily on volatile assets being at a good price at the exact time it is needed.

This creates a separate liquidity question for the portfolio.


---

## Then We Looked at the Portfolio

The 21 holdings were broken down by:

- Asset class
- Geography
- Economic exposure
- Portfolio weight
- Return
- Risk

This was important because different investments can still carry the same underlying risk.

For example, owning several technology-related investments does not necessarily mean having several independent sources of return.


---

## What The Portfolio Actually Looks Like

The analysis found:

- **37.6%** in the top five holdings
- **11.6%** in the semiconductor cluster
- **13.8%** in the mapped US growth sleeve
- **12.7%** in fixed income
- **3.5%** in cash / liquidity

The portfolio therefore has meaningful growth exposure, but some of that exposure overlaps.


---

## The Hidden Overlap

This is where the analysis goes beyond looking at individual holdings.

SOXX and NVIDIA, for example, are separate holdings.

But both are exposed to the semiconductor industry.

The same idea applies to QQQ, Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and other technology exposures.

So the analysis groups holdings by their underlying economic or thematic exposure instead of treating every security as completely independent.


---

## Performance

The workbook then looks at where the portfolio's return is coming from.

It includes:

- Holding-level returns
- Contribution to portfolio return
- Portfolio performance
- Volatility
- Benchmark comparisons
- Market data

The aim is not just to find the highest-returning investment.

It is to understand **which parts of the portfolio are driving the result and what risks came with that return.**


---

## Market Context

The portfolio was compared with relevant market benchmarks using a common market-date framework.

The analysis includes references such as:

- Nifty 50
- Nifty 500
- Nifty Midcap 150
- Nifty Bank
- S&P 500
- Nasdaq-100
- SOXX
- Bitcoin
- Other relevant market series

This gives the portfolio a market context rather than looking at its returns in isolation.


---

## Stress Testing

The portfolio was also tested under adverse scenarios.

The purpose was simple:

**What could a bad market environment do to the portfolio?**

The stress analysis looks at the impact of movements in areas such as:

- Equities
- Technology
- Semiconductors
- Crypto
- Other major portfolio exposures

This helps connect portfolio risk with the client's actual goals.


---

## Red Flags

The analysis brings the findings together into a set of portfolio red flags.

### Theme concentration

SOXX + NVIDIA create a meaningful semiconductor exposure.

### Technology overlap

QQQ, MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN and other technology holdings create overlapping exposure.

### Crypto volatility

Crypto is not the largest part of the portfolio, but its volatility makes it more important for shorter-term goals.

### Single-stock concentration

Individual holdings can create more risk when combined with exposure to the same company through ETFs or funds.

### Liquidity

The portfolio has debt, fixed deposits and liquid assets, but the exact matching of those assets to the ₹35.4 lakh requirement still needs to be verified.

### Data gaps

Some client information would need to be confirmed before making a final recommendation.


---

## From Analysis To Action

The final part of the workbook turns the findings into decisions.

Each major issue is considered against the client's goals.

The recommendations are built around three simple actions:

**Keep** — where the holding still fits the client's needs.

**Review** — where concentration, overlap or risk needs attention.

**Ring-fence** — where money is needed for a specific future requirement.
